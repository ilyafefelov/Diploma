## What the state of the art actually looks like

The forecasting layer of the state of the art is no longer just “pick a deep model.” Recent EPF research emphasizes **probabilistic, multi-horizon, covariate-aware, market-aware forecasting** rather than pure point-forecast accuracy. NBEATSx remains highly relevant because it was built specifically to incorporate exogenous variables for electricity price forecasting and reported state-of-the-art gains over prior EPF approaches. TFT remains relevant because it explicitly handles static covariates, known future inputs, and past observed inputs in interpretable multi-horizon forecasting. At the same time, the literature has become more skeptical of assuming that transformer-style models are automatically best for all time-series tasks: N-HiTS showed strong long-horizon performance and major efficiency advantages, while the LTSF-Linear / DLinear line showed that very simple linear baselines can outperform many transformer forecasters on benchmark tasks. Recent EPF reviews therefore emphasize taxonomy, probabilistic outputs, and market-specific modeling rather than one universal “best model.” citeturn9academia0turn1academia0turn24academia0turn24academia1turn4academia0turn9academia3

The frontier beyond that is moving toward **electricity-specific foundation models** and broader time-series foundation models, but this is still a frontier, not a settled production recipe for your use case. PriceFM is a strong example of the new direction: a spatiotemporal foundation model for probabilistic electricity price forecasting over multiple European regions. But the same recent literature also shows an important caveat: generic time-series foundation models often need adaptation to use covariates well, and work on volatile or sparse domains still suggests that feature-engineered or domain-adapted supervised models can outperform foundation models outside their sweet spots. For a DAM-first academic MVP, this means TSFMs are a **research lane**, not the default baseline lane. citeturn3academia1turn3academia0turn10academia1turn4academia2

The decision layer of the state of the art has advanced in a clear sequence. The classic starting point is SPO / SPO+, which formalized the idea that prediction should be trained against downstream decision quality rather than only pointwise forecast error. More recent DFL work for energy storage arbitrage then moved from this general principle into application-specific formulations: surrogate-regret learning for ESS price prediction, perturbation-based DFL for storage dispatch, fully differentiable multistage forecasting for arbitrage, and finally tri-layer predict-then-bid systems that include market clearing. That sequence is important because it gives you a roadmap, not just a citation list. citeturn19academia0turn0academia2turn11academia3turn11academia0turn11academia1turn11academia2

The control / policy-learning frontier is different again. Decision Transformer showed that offline RL can be posed as sequence modeling conditioned on return-to-go, and later work found that DT can be appealing in sparse-reward and long-horizon settings. But the same literature also found that DT generally needs more data than CQL to become competitive. For a research prototype with limited and still-gated sequence data, DT is usually not the first lane to promote to the thesis headline. It is a legitimate research lane, but usually a *later* lane. citeturn21academia0turn0academia3

Finally, the physical-modeling side matters more than many forecasting-only projects admit. Reviews of lithium-ion battery modeling for power-system techno-economic studies show that overly simple power-energy models can misestimate feasible flexibility, degradation, and profitability. More detailed grid-scale battery digital-twin work also shows that degradation heterogeneity and thermal-management choices materially affect long-run performance. That does **not** mean your MVP must implement a full digital twin now. It means the literature supports your current choice to keep the present system at “feasibility-and-economics preview,” while treating full digital-twin control as a later stage requiring telemetry and stronger validation. citeturn16academia1turn16academia2turn15academia0turn17academia2turn16academia0

## What full full-DFL should mean in your context

For your project, “full full-DFL” should mean something much more precise than “we used regret in evaluation” or “we rank schedules by value.” The minimum bar is this:

- The **training target** is downstream decision quality, not only forecast error.
- The **optimization problem** that maps forecasts to schedules is inside the learning story, either through a differentiable layer, a mathematically justified surrogate such as SPO+, or a perturbation-based construction.
- The model is learning parameters that materially affect the *decision*, not merely choosing among a fixed, externally generated set of schedules.
- If you want to claim intertemporal decision quality, the architecture should account for **multistage effects** like SOC carryover, not just one-shot daily ranking.
- If you want to claim **strategic bidding**, you need the market-clearing layer too. citeturn19academia0turn11academia3turn11academia0turn11academia1turn11academia2turn1academia1

That definition also lets you separate two different end states.

The first is **full schedule-level DFL within thesis scope**. In this version, you stay DAM-only and operator-facing, but the model is genuinely trained against downstream arbitrage regret or value through a differentiable or surrogate decision layer. This is the most realistic “full DFL” target for the thesis line. It fits the literature on SPO+, surrogate-regret ESS arbitrage, perturbation-based DFL, and multistage differentiable forecasting. citeturn19academia0turn11academia3turn11academia0turn11academia1

The second is **full strategic bid DFL beyond thesis scope**. In this version, the learning loop includes not only storage optimization but also the market-clearing mechanism. That is what the recent predict-then-bid literature does. If you are not modeling a differentiable or surrogate-clearing layer, then you should not claim that level of DFL. This is exactly why the current DAM recommendation preview boundary is correct: it keeps you from slipping from “offline recommendation” into “market-submittable bid intelligence” without the literature-grade machinery needed to justify that leap. citeturn11academia2

A related point: **full autonomy is not the same thing as full DFL**. Even if you reach full schedule-level DFL, a live autonomous system would still require non-ML controls: hard safety envelopes, execution gating, telemetry, fail-safe switching, human override, and a richer physical model of the battery. Your docs already distinguish these layers, and the battery literature strongly supports keeping that distinction. fileciteturn16file0L1-L3 citeturn16academia1turn15academia0turn16academia2

## Recommended path from the current MVP to full DFL

The best path is a **staged promotion path**, not a single leap.

First, keep the thesis headline exactly where it is strongest: **DAM recommendation preview + offline evidence system**. Strengthen that stage by making the evaluation indisputable: rolling-origin backtests, strict oracle-regret evaluation, frozen baseline comparison, feasibility checks, fallback logging, and reproducible evidence packets. Your project docs already define the underlying concepts for this style of evaluation—oracle benchmark, regret, read model, offline strategy promotion, fallback, and no-live-execution boundary—so the work here is hardening, not inventing. fileciteturn16file0L1-L3

Second, finish the **source-readiness gate** before any stronger DFL claim. In practical terms, this means completing safe-switch examples, provenance/receipt coverage, and the source checks needed to trust the data-generating surface. Scientific point: DFL is *more* brittle than forecast-only learning when the labels, exogenous inputs, or state transitions are unreliable, because the optimization layer amplifies bad structure. This is an inference from the literature and your status note, but it is the right inference. The DFL survey makes clear that method behavior is highly problem- and setup-dependent, which strengthens the case for getting the data substrate right before upgrading the claim. citeturn0academia2

Third, expand the forecast lane in a disciplined way. For an academic MVP, I would keep a **small but very strong forecast set**: one strong domain model such as NBEATSx, one strong multi-horizon covariate-aware model such as TFT, one strong long-horizon alternative such as N-HiTS, and one deceptively strong simple baseline such as DLinear / linear autoregression. Require probabilistic or quantile-capable outputs where possible, because modern EPF practice increasingly values calibrated uncertainty, not just point forecasts. Do **not** let foundation models become the mainline unless they clearly beat these supervised baselines under your rolling-origin protocol. citeturn9academia0turn1academia0turn24academia0turn24academia1turn4academia0turn3academia1turn3academia0

Fourth, move from “decision-aware evaluation” to **surrogate DFL**. The cleanest next step is a DAM-only parametric arbitrage problem, where prices or latent decision-relevant parameters are predicted, then fed into a schedule optimizer, and the training loss becomes a hybrid of forecast loss and decision loss. The literature gives you several viable recipes here: SPO+, surrogate-regret for ESS arbitrage, and perturbation-based methods for making storage optimization differentiable enough for training. This stage would still be thesis-safe because it can remain offline, DAM-only, and non-executing while being much closer to true DFL. citeturn19academia0turn11academia3turn11academia0turn1academia1

Fifth, if you want the strong “full DFL” claim for the thesis line, promote to **multistage differentiable arbitrage**. This is where SOC transitions, horizon coupling, and state-path effects become first-class citizens in the training architecture rather than being handled only by ex post evaluation. The Peršak–Anjos formulation is the clearest academic template here: not just a one-step decision loss, but a recursive differentiable multistage optimization view. Reaching this stage is what would let you say, with a straight face, that you have a *full schedule-level DFL system* rather than a decision-aware selector. citeturn11academia1

Sixth, keep the **Decision Transformer lane separate** until you have enough trajectory data. DT is best treated as a parallel research lane for offline sequential policy learning after you have a large, reliable corpus of state-action-return trajectories and stable action semantics. The literature is encouraging about DT in long-horizon and sparse-reward settings, but it is also explicit that DT generally needs more data than CQL to be competitive. Given the source-readiness gating you described, DT should remain a later-stage research probe, not the basis of the thesis claim right now. citeturn21academia0turn0academia3

Seventh, separate **strategic bid optimization** and **live execution** into post-thesis gates. Strategic bid DFL requires market clearing in the learning story. Live execution additionally requires operational governance that sits outside the DFL literature: operator override, telemetry, digital-twin or at least richer battery-state monitoring, conservative action envelopes, and market/protocol compliance. That is a different milestone, and your current project language is right to keep it distinct. citeturn11academia2turn16academia1turn15academia0

A concise claim ladder would look like this:

- **Now:** decision-aware DAM recommendation preview with offline strategy evidence and strict LP/oracle evaluation.
- **Next:** DAM-only surrogate-DFL trained against regret/value.
- **Then:** full multistage differentiable DFL for price-to-schedule learning.
- **Later:** strategic bid DFL with market-clearing layer.
- **Only after separate gates:** live market execution and deployed control. fileciteturn18file0L1-L3 citeturn11academia1turn11academia2turn11academia3turn19academia0
