\begin{abstract}
Battery energy storage system (BESS) arbitrage is often approached as an electricity-price forecasting problem, although forecast error is only indirectly related to the economic quality of a charge-discharge schedule. A prediction can have an acceptable average error and still misorder the few hours that determine arbitrage value, violate state-of-charge timing, or induce excessive throughput. This retrospective study presents an evidence-oriented pipeline for evaluating hourly BESS recommendations for Ukraine's day-ahead market (DAM). Forecast and heuristic candidates are converted into feasible schedules and scored in a common linear-programming (LP) and oracle contour. Regret is the value lost relative to the schedule optimized with realized prices. The main descriptive window contains 18 distinct dates crossed with five configured BESS profiles, yielding 90 paired profile-date rows that share one DAM price process. It is also the latest of four adjacent rolling windows, not an additional independent test.

Raw and horizon-calibrated NBEATSx are reported as co-primary sensitivity sources because their relative status was assessed on the same retrospective window. With calibrated NBEATSx, Schedule/Value Learner V2+ had mean regret 174.77 UAH, versus 310.58 UAH for the strict comparator and 206.37 UAH for V2; the raw-source V2+ mean was 193.36 UAH, versus 310.58 and 225.44 UAH. The V2+ mean was below both comparators in four adjacent rolling windows for both sources. These are descriptive estimates, not confirmatory holdout results. A lineage audit found that a later artifact historically named \code{dt_v2_plus} is a random forest trained on exact timestamp-shifted copies of the evaluation packet. A 14-row time-separated RF suite has no improvement over V2+. A separate 36-run, return-conditioned Decision Transformer suite has zero train/evaluation content overlap: a decision-aware objective ties V2+ in 18/18 runs, while candidate-index cross entropy ties in 15/18 and is harmful in 3/18. Finally, a preregistered 72-run differentiable forecast-to-storage suite backpropagates through profile-specific \code{cvxpylayers} problems and then uses strict LP/oracle rescoring. No run beats V2+. Nevertheless, transformer correction has lower strict regret than a matched MLP in 28/36 comparisons and the forecast-loss transformer improves on the raw schedule in 15/18 runs. This is positive architecture evidence and negative promotion evidence, not a V2+ replacement.

The contribution is architectural and evidentiary: a reproducible separation of forecast quality, schedule value, shadow-model evidence, source governance, and execution readiness. No new forecasting, optimization, or sequence-model method is claimed. Intraday-market (IDM) support is an hourly read-model capability, not evaluated bidding performance, and market execution remains disabled.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords---}\ battery energy storage system; energy arbitrage; day-ahead market; decision-focused evaluation; regret; rolling-origin evaluation; evidence governance.
